    The lake, and how to keep it healthy

    Everything that lands on a lawn or a driveway around here ends up in the same basin. A small lake has little water to dilute it, so what each household does shows up in the water within a season.

    Go easy on lawn fertilizer

    Phosphorus that washes off a lawn feeds algae. Use a phosphorus-free product, apply less than the bag says, and skip it when rain is forecast.

    Keep clippings and leaves out of the water

    Grass and leaves rot on the bottom, release nutrients, and use up oxygen fish need. Mulch them on the lawn or bag them, and blow away from the shore.

    Respect shoreline erosion zones

    Stay off bare or slumping banks and keep mowers back from the edge. A strip of taller native plants along the water holds soil better than turf.

    Pick up after pets

    Pet waste carries bacteria and nutrients straight to the lake with the next rain.

    Nothing down the storm drains

    Street drains empty into the lake untreated. That includes soapy water from car washing, paint rinse, and pool water.

    Never release anything into the lake

    Bait buckets, aquarium fish, and pond plants can bring in species that are close to impossible to remove.

    Fishing guide and catch log

    Five kinds of fish live here. Logging what you catch tells us how the population is doing, which is how fair size limits get set.

    Bass

    Largemouth, Micropterus salmoides

    The top predator. Look along weed edges, fallen timber, and docks early and late in the day.

    Catfish

    Channel catfish, Ictalurus punctatus

    Bottom feeders that bite best at dusk and after dark, often in the deeper water toward the dam.

    Carp

    Common carp, Cyprinus carpio

    Big, strong, and wary. They root in the shallows and stir up sediment, which clouds the water.

    Sunfish

    Bluegill and relatives, Lepomis

    Plentiful near shore and the best first fish for kids. A small hook and a bit of worm is all it takes.

    Crappie

    Pomoxis

    School around submerged brush. Spring is the easiest time to find them shallow.

    Catch-and-release guidelines Draft for board review

    • Release all bass for now. Large bass keep sunfish numbers in check. A slot limit will be set once the catch log shows what sizes are out there.
    • Wet your hands before handling a fish, support its belly, and get it back in the water quickly. Measure and photograph first, then release.
    • Pinch down barbs when you plan to release. Cut the line on a deeply hooked fish; do not pull the hook.
    • Keeping a few sunfish or catfish for the table is fine. Take only what you will eat.
    • Pack out all line, hooks, and bait containers. Loose line tangles turtles and birds.
    • Never empty a bait bucket into the lake.

    Who else lives here

    A field guide to the animals you are likely to meet along the shoreline, and what to do when you meet them.

    Two rules cover almost everything: do not feed the geese or any other wildlife, and watch from a distance. Keep dogs leashed near the water, especially in spring when birds are nesting.

    Red-eared slider

    Trachemys scripta elegans

    The turtles lined up on logs on sunny days. They slide off when approached. Females cross lawns in late spring to nest; let them pass.

    Harmless

    Canada goose

    Branta canadensis

    Resident year-round. Feeding them brings larger flocks, and their droppings load the lake with nutrients. Nesting pairs will charge to defend a nest.

    Do not feed

    Ducks

    Mallards and wood ducks

    Dabble in the shallows and nest in cover near shore. Bread harms them and fouls the water.

    Do not feed

    Red fox

    Vulpes vulpes

    Seen at dawn and dusk hunting mice and rabbits. Shy around people. Bring small pets in at night and keep pet food indoors.

    Keeps its distance

    Raccoon

    Procyon lotor

    Works the shoreline at night for crawfish and mussels. Secure trash lids. One that is out in daylight and acting unwell should be left alone and reported.

    Do not approach

    Muskrat

    Ondatra zibethicus

    A small swimmer with a thin tail, often mistaken for a beaver. It burrows into banks, and burrows in the dam can weaken it.

    Report burrows near the dam

    Crawfish

    Family Cambaridae

    Under rocks in the shallows, and the builders of the small mud chimneys you see on wet ground near shore. Food for bass, raccoons, and herons.

    Harmless

    Native snakes

    Watersnakes and garter snakes

    Most snakes near the water are non-venomous and eat fish, frogs, and rodents. Any snake may bite if grabbed. Step back and it will leave.

    Leave it alone

    Rules and safety Draft for board review

    These are proposed starting points. The association's recorded covenants govern wherever they differ.

    Shoreline access

    • The lake is for residents and their accompanied guests.
    • Reach the water from your own lot or a common access point. Do not cross a neighbor's yard without permission.
    • Stay off the dam face and away from the spillway.
    • Leave shoreline plants and rock in place; they are holding the bank.

    Boats and paddle craft

    • Non-motorized craft only: kayaks, canoes, paddleboards, and rowboats.
    • One life jacket per person on board. Children wear theirs.
    • Clean, drain, and dry any boat that has been on other water before it goes in here.
    • Store boats above the bank, not on it.

    On and near the water

    • There is no lifeguard. Children need an adult within reach.
    • Ice here is never checked and never guaranteed safe.
    • Get off the water at the first thunder.
    • Stay out of water that is green, scummy, or smells bad, and keep pets out too. Then report it below.

    In an emergency

    Call 911 first for anyone in danger, or for water going over or through the dam. Use the form below afterward so the lake manager has a record.
